First: Choose Your Game Genre
Some genres are inherently more accessible and addictive:

Hyper-casual games — one-tap mechanics (like Flappy Bird, Subway Surfers). Simple controls, short sessions, huge appeal.
Puzzle games — match-3, block puzzles (Tetris, Candy Crush). Easy to learn, hard to put down.
Endless runners — tap to jump/switch lanes. Instant fun.
Physics-based games — Angry Birds style. Satisfying and intuitive.
For broad age appeal, simple one-touch controls and short play sessions (2-5 minutes) are the key factors.

The Psychology of "Addictive"
What actually makes games addictive:

Quick reward loops — constant small wins (points, coins, level-ups)
Just-one-more-try — short failures that feel instantly retryable
Progression — visible progress (levels, ranks, unlocks)
Randomness — variable rewards trigger dopamine (like slot machines)
Social elements — leaderboards, sharing scores, challenges

Development Roadmap
Prototype the core mechanic first — build the single most fun action in 2 weeks. If it's not fun, change the concept.
Never skip the juice — screen shake, particle effects, sound effects, animations. These small polish touches are what make games feel good and addictive.
Add progression — levels, unlockables, daily rewards.
Test with real people — especially non-gamers and different ages.
Iterate based on "fun factor", not features.
